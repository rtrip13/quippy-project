# UNLABELED beta protocol

## Goal

Validate whether incoming students move from a recommendation to one real-world test of the work. The recommendation is a hypothesis, never an aptitude judgment.

## Pilot

- 10–20 incoming students, beginning with Michigan.
- 2–4 academic advisors for workflow and language review.
- Ask participants to use their own phone and think aloud only during the first session.
- Follow up after 7 and 14 days; do not manufacture streaks or daily engagement.

## Session script

1. Start without explaining the scoring model.
2. Observe whether the student reaches the reveal without assistance.
3. Ask: “What do you think the app noticed?”
4. Have the student open one result, save one alternative, and compare two options.
5. Ask them to choose one fieldwork activity they could genuinely complete.
6. After fieldwork, capture energized/neutral/drained before discussing the result.
7. Verify that the student understands why the ranking changed—or did not change.

## Success metrics

Primary metric: percentage of activated students who complete one real fieldwork activity within 14 days.

Supporting metrics:

- onboarding completion and time to reveal;
- result-open and shortlist-save rates;
- comparison started/completed;
- mission started, reflection submitted, and return session;
- recommendation usefulness: accurate, surprising, useful;
- advisor report usefulness.

Guardrails:

- recommendation diversity across broad fields;
- percentage who mistake fit for ability or admission odds;
- persistence failures and abandoned resume attempts;
- accessibility blockers;
- requests to delete or reset progress.

## Interview prompts

- What felt specific to you? What felt generic?
- Which recommendation would you actually test? Why?
- What evidence would change your mind?
- Did any wording feel judgmental or too certain?
- What would make sharing this with an advisor useful?
- What information was missing when comparing majors?

## Privacy

- Do not collect raw freeform notes or individual profile answers in analytics.
- Do not record names, email addresses, or student IDs for product analytics.
- Keep research consent and scheduling information outside the app dataset.
- Let students reset local progress at any time.
